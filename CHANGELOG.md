# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Quarter-end close checklist, from chat or the page
- **Ask for a close checklist and the app builds and keeps it.** The prompt people paste into an assistant ("build a quarter-end close checklist for a venture fund with 18 portfolio companies, administrator outsourced, LP reporting due November 16") is now a tool, `close_checklist_create`, and a prompt in the assistant's menu, **Quarter-end close checklist**. It saves tasks grouped by owner (fund administrator, portfolio companies, internal team), each due a set number of days before LP delivery, rolled back to a business day and compressed in proportion when the window is short. It names the task that blocks the most downstream work (collecting the portfolio companies' financials, as built) and, as work completes, the open task now holding up the most. PE funds add covenant certificates and the credit facility; an in-house team takes the administrator's tasks; many companies get more lead time (`lib/close-checklist/template.ts`).
- **"Tied out" is its own column.** A task's status says the work is finished; its tie-out says a second person agreed its numbers to source. Only a done task can be tied out, the person who completed it cannot tie it out (an admin may, with a note), an exception needs a note, and reopening a tied-out task un-ties it. Who completed and who tied out each task are recorded, and every change is in the audit trail with its source (`close_checklist.*` actions).
- **Work it anywhere.** `close_checklist`, `close_checklist_list`, `close_checklist_update_tasks`, `close_checklist_add_task` and `close_checklist_update` (moving the delivery date moves every task without its own date), the `/api/accounting/close-checklists` routes, and a **Close checklist** page at `/funds/close-checklist` all run one service (`lib/close-checklist/service.ts`), gated on Fund accounting. From chat, building and ticking off tasks apply directly, since they change no books; a tie-out is staged for approval like any other write. Migration `20261013150000_close_checklists.sql` adds `close_checklists` and `close_checklist_tasks` (service role only); push it before deploying.

### LP names open their capital statement from LPs → Capital accounts
- **LP names on LPs → Capital accounts now link to the statement.** On a ledger-backed vehicle, each LP name on LPs → Capital accounts now links to that LP's capital statement, where Generate PDF lives, and the statement's back link returns to the LPs page. Before, the names there were plain text and the statement was reachable only from Funds → Capital accounts. The link shows only to members who can open the statement (`app/(app)/lps/capital/view.tsx`).

### Complete ledger reads for assistants
- **An assistant's view of the books no longer stops at 1000 rows without saying so.** `export_ledger_text` asked for 2000 entries but PostgREST returns at most 1000, so it exported only the oldest 1000 and the newest entries were missing. A reconciliation built on that export showed $547,832 of LP capital as unassigned when it was not. The export now reads every entry in pages (`fetchAllRows`), takes `start`, `end` and `asOf`, and splits a very large ledger into pages marked `truncated`, with a `cursor` to continue (`lib/accounting/text-ledger-run.ts`). The same full read fixes the period-close snapshot. `list_journal` and `list_bank_transactions` now return `{ rows, total, hasMore, nextCursor }` instead of a bare array that ended at its limit. `bank_reconciliation`, the LP entity, commitment-event and `lp_investments` reads behind `list_entities`, `capital_accounts`, `lp_commitments` and `list_lps`, and the allocation basis in `loadOwnership` also read every row now (`lib/accounting/agent-tools.ts`, `lib/accounting/load.ts`, `lib/agent/lp-tools.ts`, `lib/agent/lp-record-tools.ts`, `lib/agent/portfolio-tools.ts`). A REST caller of `list_journal` or `list_bank_transactions` must now read `.rows`.
### Partner allocation happens exactly once
- **The close no longer allocates P&L a second time.** Its catch-up read which entries were already allocated in one unpaged query, so on an entity with more than 1,000 allocated entries the rest looked unallocated and were allocated again; the same 1,000-row cut also truncated the rounding carried between allocations. Those reads are paged now (`lib/accounting/close.ts`, `lib/accounting/continuous-allocation.ts`), and a failed read refuses the preview instead of reading as "nothing allocated". Posting with allocation off (`allocate: false`, used for QuickBooks and Carta imports whose books already carry each partner's capital lines) is recorded, and a compound entry that carries its own allocation lines (3200 and partner capital, ACCOUNTING.md entries 4–7) is recognised when it posts and not allocated again. That second allocation once credited $37,820 of interest twice. Both are recorded in the new `journal_entry_allocation_skips` table, and the close leaves those entries alone. An imported entry with neither an allocation nor a record is listed as a close warning and never allocated silently. Migration `20261013140000_journal_entry_allocation_skips.sql` creates the table and backfills it from the audited `allocate: false` bulk posts; push it before deploying.
- **An exit's Proceeds means the gross, everywhere.** The form asks for gross proceeds next to *In escrow*, and the tracker read it that way, but the ledger booked Proceeds as the cash received and added the escrow on top, so an exit with a holdback overstated cash and realized gain by the escrow. One definition now (`lib/portfolio/proceeds.ts`): `proceeds_received` is gross, `proceeds_escrow` is the part of it held back. An exit books gross − escrow to cash, the escrow to 1350 and gross − basis as gain (`exitPostings` in `lib/accounting/from-portfolio.ts`); the realized-gains and K-1 reports, LP letters, the portfolio API, the AI context and bank import review read it the same way, adoption stores the gross (`lib/accounting/adopt.ts`), and an escrow larger than the proceeds is refused. `record_investment` and `update_investment` say so and take `proceeds_escrow`. Posted entries are not re-booked: an exit with escrow booked the old way is listed on the entity's status page and by `scripts/diagnose-escrow-exits.ts` for checking against the bank, and re-books only when someone edits it. `supabase/pending-deploy/adopted_exit_proceeds_gross.sql` restates adopted exits (stored net) as gross, optionally, after this release is live.

### Headless: chat and scripts as first-class ways in and out
- **Chat writes wait for approval.** A change Claude or ChatGPT makes over OAuth is staged as a pending action and applied only when a person approves it, under the approver's own access. API-key writes apply directly. One dispatcher (`lib/agent/dispatch.ts`) serves `/api/mcp` and `/api/agent`, with idempotency keys (`_meta.idempotencyKey`, `Idempotency-Key`).
- **One approval per run, and a chat cannot approve its own changes.** A new MCP tool, `stage_run`, stages a whole fix as one pending action: a title, a rationale, the ordered write-tool steps (each validated against its tool's schema and the caller's access when staged) and optional expected checks (an account balance, a type total or the trial balance balancing, for a vehicle as of a date). Approving it in the app runs the steps in order under the approver's access, stops at the first failure and records where, then reads the checks. A `post_drafts` step follows its cursor, so posting every draft is one step. Approvals shows each run's steps, checks and results, and **Undo** voids the entries it created and deletes the investment transactions it recorded where that is safe; other steps are listed as not undoable. Before, one fix took dozens of approvals (44 for a holdings correction), so threads fell back to SQL. `approve_action` and `/api/v1` now refuse an approval from the credential that staged the change, and the assistant hands the person the Approvals link instead; the app approves as before. New `lib/agent/run-action.ts`, `run-checks.ts`, `run-undo.ts`, `components/agent-run-card.tsx`, `/api/pending-actions/[id]/undo`; migration `20261013143000_pending_actions_staged_credential.sql`.
- **The same capital posting cannot be booked twice from chat.** Two chat sessions had each booked the same carry reallocation ($209,207.98), and an opening-capital bootstrap had been posted twice ($1.83M). The `allocation` tool now stamps each entry with a ref derived from its action, date and lines and refuses a live copy, naming it (migration `20261013141500_agent_allocation_source_ref_once.sql` adds the unique index). The bootstrap (`lib/accounting/bootstrap.ts`) refuses when the partners already have capital on the books and uses the `partner-opening` ref, so the per-date index catches a race. `post_ledger_text` now writes the `ref:` it checks, so re-sending the same text is refused; it checks closed periods before writing anything, and stops at the first failed write, listing the entries already in. A chat assistant's write that would be staged and has no idempotency key is keyed by its tool and input for 24 hours (`lib/agent/dispatch.ts`), so asking again, from any chat, returns the first pending action. Draft-only calls are not deduplicated this way. Existing duplicates are not touched and need voiding by hand.
- **Every change records its source.** The audit trail and activity log say whether a change came from `web`, `api` or `mcp`. Migration `20261012140000_audit_events_source.sql`.
- **Admin-only changes on every surface.** Deletes, undoing posted entries and reopening periods, capital calls and distributions, anything sent to LPs, finalizing and delivering K-1s, reopening a tax year and the fund's structure need an admin in the app, over the API and over MCP (`lib/access/admin-only.ts`). A member can propose one from chat.
- **Tax area, presets and end dates.** Tax is its own access area, needing read on the books (migration `20261012150000_tax_domain.sql` copies each accounting grant into it). The access grid applies presets (CFO, fund administrator, tax preparer, auditor, investment team, investor relations) and an *Access ends* date, enforced in `access_context` and swept by the hourly `/api/cron/access-expiry` job (migration `20261012160000_member_access_expiry.sql`).
- **Set up a fund without the web app.** `npm run bootstrap` creates the account, the fund and an API key from a terminal; the OAuth consent screen creates a fund for someone who has none. The admin-only `setup_status` and `setup_fund` tools and the **Set up this fund** prompt add products, entities, team members with their access, and authorized senders in one approved call. README, DOCS and FEATURES describe the headless path.

- **A conversation can reach a tool added after it started.** Chat hosts cache the MCP tool list per conversation, and the stateless server cannot announce a change, so a new tool such as `update_investment` was unusable until the member started a new chat. Two fixed tools now cover that: `search_tools` searches the current list, filtered to the member's access as `tools/list` is, and `call_tool` runs a tool by name through the same path as a direct call (`lib/mcp-apps/gateway.ts`, `lib/mcp-apps/call.ts`), with the same refusals, approvals, rate limit, audit and idempotency key. `initialize` and `tools/list` carry a `toolListVersion`, and the server instructions point the assistant at both tools. `/api/agent` refuses them, since its list is never cached.

- **Connected assistants can correct an investment transaction.** New `update_investment` and `delete_investment` tools edit or delete a recorded transaction by id, such as turning a mark into an FX revaluation. Before, an assistant could only add one. They run the same code as the company page's edit and delete (`lib/portfolio/transaction-edit.ts`): the journal entry is retracted first and re-derived from the new values, and the same refusals apply (closed period, fund-register rows, conversion sources). Over OAuth they are staged for approval like any other write.

### Data connectors
- **Outside general ledgers can be fed by a connector, not just a pasted export.** The QuickBooks migration's three steps are now agent tools over MCP and REST: `journal_import_accounts` (the accounts a batch touches, with the saved mapping or a proposal; writes nothing), `save_journal_mapping` and `import_journal` (drafts for review, nothing posts). They take structured transactions as well as the Journal export text, and run the same code as **Accounting → QuickBooks** (`lib/accounting/quickbooks/import-journal.ts`). Structured transactions pass the same balance check as a parsed export; an amount that can't be read is refused, not read as zero.
- **Carta's fund-admin ledger uses the same import.** A `source` (`quickbooks` or `carta`, `lib/accounting/quickbooks/sources.ts`) keeps each book's account mapping separate. It tags the entries it creates (`source_type`, and `carta:` idempotency refs). Carta entries get the treatment QuickBooks entries always had: the LP roll-forward reads their cash and capital exchanges, and bank import matches against their cash lines. Migration `20261012210000_journal_import_sources.sql` adds `source` to `qb_account_mappings` and `qb_import_runs`; push it before deploying. `20261012220000_journal_import_sources_contract.sql` then drops the old three-column unique constraint; apply it only after this release is live.
- **The journal import no longer drops a payment because another payer sent the same amount that day.** The duplicate check hashed date, type, number, accounts and amounts but not who paid, so two LPs contributing the same amount on the same day with no transaction number got one ref, and every one after the first was skipped as already imported (on one real fund file, 52 contributions, about $1.5M). Refs now also hash each line's name and memo and the transaction memo (`qb:v2:` / `carta:v2:`), and identical transactions in one batch are numbered rather than collided. Entries already imported under the old refs keep them: an incoming transaction also counts as present when it pairs with one, by count, preferring the old entry with the same LP or vendor and never one for a different LP or vendor (`lib/accounting/quickbooks/dedupe.ts`). Re-running an old file adds nothing; re-running the file that lost contributions adds only the missing ones. The import result and the Migrate page now list each transaction treated as already present, with the entry it matched and why, and each recovered one. A voided entry still blocks re-import of its transaction, as before.
- **`examples/connector-template`.** A starting point for a private connector repository: an MCP client for the deployment, a runner (windowing with overlap, batching, review holds that stop the run, a cursor), OAuth helpers for sources that are themselves MCP servers, and a file-based example. See DOCS → Data connectors.

- **Company updates from chat.** `submit_company_update` takes an update's text or small files (up to five, 3 MB each) and runs the emailed-update pipeline on it: company match, the company's Updates, KPI extraction, and the review queue for anything uncertain. It is recorded as an inbound email routed to reporting, so it can be reprocessed from the Emails page. Files are scanned before they are stored.

- **Approval preview for updates.** The approver sees file names and sizes, not their bytes. A member scoped to some entities can stage a change about a company linked to one of them. The MCP, agent and approval routes now allow 120 seconds, the same as inbound email. An update that takes longer than 45 seconds returns a job and keeps processing (see below).

- **Deals and diligence from chat and the API.** Fourteen new write tools cover inbound deals (create, move, research, promote to diligence) and the deal room (create and update, checklist items, notes, documents, the attention queue, running the memo agent, writing in the memo, and finalizing it). The web routes they mirror now call shared functions (`lib/deals/service.ts`, `lib/diligence/service.ts`, `lib/memo-agent/enqueue.ts`), so both doors apply the same entity rules. Finalizing a memo joins the admin-only list. `diligence_memo` now returns paragraph and attention-item ids for the tools to act on.

### Questions and long-running work from chat

### Forecast — one plan (phase 1 of `plans/plan-unified-plan.md`)
- **A forecast plan holds its own portfolio construction.** Each plan — budgets included — carries the deals, exits, reserves and pacing its scenario assumes, beside its fund forecast. One plan per entity is **primary** (the entity's headline figures, a management company's fee income and a GP entity's share read it). The construction page and the Analyst's construction changes now save onto the primary plan (made — "Base case" — if the entity has none), and every plan compiles on its own construction, so a downside case can have its own exits. Until a plan has been given its own, it reads the entity's existing construction model, so nothing changes before the first save. Published versions freeze both halves. Migration `20261010400000_forecast_plan_construction.sql` (push before deploying: the forecast reads the new columns).
- **Fees and expenses are the forecast's (phase 2).** A fund's management fee is a rule on its plan — *Management fee*: a rate on committed, invested or NAV capital for a term, with an optional step-down — charged on the fee-paying commitments (the GP's own stake pays none) and paid on the fee link's cycle when the fund has one. Expenses are ordinary rules. Portfolio construction's capital planning (investable capital, calls) now reads the plan's fees and expenses instead of its own inputs, which leave the construction page (it shows the figures and links to Forecast); an entity without a plan keeps its earlier construction terms until it has one. A new plan is given a fee rule, a partnership-expense rule and the remaining org costs from those terms, on any account without its own rule, and rule suggestions offer the fee rule instead of the old construction link. A management company's fee income reads each fund's primary-plan fee. The fee rule charges exactly what construction did, year by year. Migration `20261010410000_forecast_management_fee_rule.sql`.
- **Distributions broken down on the cash-flow statement.** Distributions stay one line (where a month can still be typed), with *To the LPs*, *To the GP, on its own commitment* and *Carried interest* beneath it. The forecast books the LPs' and the GP's shares as separate entries; posted actuals are split by each partner's class. A held company's hold period is years from its first investment to its exit — the edit dialog and the Analyst's schema now say so (they said "from today").
- **Forecast exits realize the mark; prepaid fees can be refunded.** A forecast exit of a company the fund holds now books to that company's own accounts and reverses its unrealized appreciation (and currency translation) on the books against their income lines, so the whole gain over cost is realized and nothing stays unrealized after the exit. A prepaid cost's rule can name a *refund month* (rule dialog → When it is paid → Already paid, or the Analyst's `cashTiming.refundMonth`): the cost stops that month and whatever is still prepaid comes back as cash, paid out to the investors with that month's distribution — for a fee prepaid for a fund's life when it winds up early. The month lives on the plan's rule in the database, not in the engine; no forecasting code names any entity.
- **The Analyst and connected agents can change all of a forecast.** The Analyst's Investments edit (`update_portfolio_construction`) now also takes the fund-wide return assumptions — hold period, default exit multiple, write-off rate, exit timing range, dispersion — alongside each deal's check, timing, exit and simulation settings; a new `set_entity_link` draft adds or changes an entity link; saving a plan reads as saving a scenario or the budget. Over MCP, `portfolio_construction_update` makes the same Investments changes directly (with write access), beside the existing forecast tools; `/api/v1/chat` gets everything the Analyst can do. The help page's Forecast section describes the page as it now is. In the Analyst panel, *New chat* clears the box (and any attached document), and a rejected draft disappears.
- **Forecast page: faster, and tidier actions.** A plan's construction is worked out once per request rather than up to four times, from the one vehicle's economics rather than every vehicle in the fund, and the page fetches the plan's returns once for both the key figures and the cash-flow range — plan detail went from about 7 s to 2.5 s and the returns from 11 s to under 3 s on a live fund. The action bar reads *Add Investment* (*Investment* while the Analyst is open), the settings menu, and *Scenario* (save it, copy it, start a new one, or draft one with AI). Capital planning follows the page's date range: calls, distributions, investment and each fee and expense actual and forecast within it, uncalled capital at its end. *Lowest cash* is gone from the key figures; the Financials hints sit above the statement switcher.
- **Investments and Capital planning, simplified; Returns tab removed.** The Investments table carries the deal-by-deal forecast: current MOIC beside current value, realized proceeds, then *Hold period* and *Forecast multiple* typed in the row (blank = the fund default), forecasted proceeds and each deal's share of them; the edit dialog gains *Invest in* and *Hold period*. Capital planning is one row per line in *Actual · Forecast · Total · % committed* columns — capital called (with the calls still to come and the year of the last), uncalled, each fee and expense paid and to pay, new and follow-on investment, and what is not yet allocated (or *Over-committed*); a line with nothing in it for the vehicle is left out. The Returns tab's simulated outcomes moved to Charts; its deal-by-deal table is gone.
- **Charts tab.** The Forecast page opens on a *Charts* tab holding every chart: cash flows and multiples by year, revenue and expenses, cash in the bank, committed capital usage, and the simulated outcomes. The other tabs keep their tables and editors. The simulated range of distributions is now month by month, so it follows exit timing at any interval.
- **Financials statements, accrual or cash basis.** Financials shows the *Income Statement*, the *Statement of Cash Flows* and *Journal Entries*, with an Accrual / Cash basis choice for both statements. The income statement on a cash basis puts each revenue and expense line in the period its cash moved, with cash that clears an earlier accrual (a payable, a prepaid fee) on a line of its own; the cash-flow statement on an accrual basis is the indirect method — net income, less investment gains, plus the change in accruals, prepaids and payables — re-adding to the same operating cash. Journal Entries now lists the books' posted entries for the actual months ahead of the forecast's own.
- **Forecast page, second pass.** Above the tabs: the key figures, then *Cash flows* (calls, distributions and carry per period, actual solid and forecast lighter, with the simulated range of distributions paid to date as a band) and *Multiples by year* (TVPI, DPI, RVPI and PIC — paid-in over committed — solid to date and dashed forecast, with the simulated TVPI range). Both follow the page's date range and Month/Quarter/Year, which now apply on every tab. Financials shows *Revenue and expenses* — operating only, investment gains left out, with a scale set by ordinary periods and outliers marked — beside *Cash in the bank*. *Committed capital usage* is a treemap with the legend beneath. *Publish* is now *Save scenario*, saved as a Scenario or a Budget; *Fee links* is *Entity links*. The forecast's notes sit behind a button beside Refresh (now an icon), an account's notes open on click, and the toolbar buttons use the muted style the rest of the app does. The Returns tab's year-by-year table is gone.
- **Forecast page refinements.** The top charts run from the actuals into the plan — calls and distributions by year (actual solid, forecast lighter, carry split out) and month-end cash (actual solid, forecast dashed) — so a call already made shows. The cash statement is a statement on Financials (P&L · Cash · Entries); Capital planning keeps commitment use and reserves, with *Committed capital usage* as a column beside its table, paid and to-come split. *Sources of total value* moved to Returns; the Returns tab's yearly table and the range figures under the cash-flow chart are gone — the chart draws the simulated range as a band of distributions paid to date (10th–90th percentile, with the median and the forecast). A draft out of date with its investments, a linked fund or the books recompiles itself when opened; the notes say what they mean.
- **Capital planning counts cash.** Its fees and expenses follow each rule's cash timing: a fee paid up front and expensed monthly needs no cash in the plan, and a cost paid later lands when it is paid. What has been paid to date counts cash too — the fees expensed plus what is still prepaid, and expenses less what is accrued but unpaid — so its rows read *Expenses paid* and *Expenses to pay*. Financials still shows the expense as it is recognised. An entity whose plans predate the primary flag reads the plan that would be picked as primary (its first active rolling forecast), so its construction uses that plan's costs before anyone marks one.
- **The simulation writes off planned deals only.** A company already held varies in its exit multiple and timing, and is written off only on a rate set for it — at the fund-wide 50% default, a one-company SPV lost everything in half the runs.
- **Called but unpaid capital is called.** Where a partner's capital comes from their statement, a call the books record against them on Due from LPs now counts as called rather than uncalled (Bluefish: uncalled was $3,523.62, Bluefish SPV Associates' unpaid call; now $0).
- **The Analyst works the plan the way the page does (phase 7, Analyst tools).** A new read tool, `forecast_returns`, gives the Analyst the figures at the top of the Forecast page and its Returns tab — gross MOIC, LP TVPI/DPI/IRR, calls, distributions and carry by year, month-end cash — with simulated ranges on request, so it can show the effect of a change by reading them before and after. Calls and distributions typed before this were hand entries; they are read as month edits. Intended change: calls and distributions are now monthly from the plan's cash rather than construction's annual figures placed by month. ### Forecast and portfolio construction
- **Construction flows now carry fees and expenses too.** With *Construction flows* on, a fund's forecast takes construction's management fees and partnership expenses on any account without its own rule (a rule — a prepaid fee, a known invoice — still wins, and says what construction would have put there).
- **A GP entity's forecast comes from its fund's.** With *Construction flows* on, a GP entity forecasts its share of the fund's capital calls and LP distributions (its commitment over the fund's) and its cut of the carry the fund's waterfall pays (its percent of the carry recipients), in the months of the fund's forecast exits — instead of from a construction model of its own.
- **A draft says when its sources have moved.** Changing portfolio construction (the entity's own, a linked fund's for a management company's fees, the fund's for a GP entity) now marks the draft out of date and offers Refresh, as a new close already did. Published versions never change.
- **The GP's own stake pays no management fee and bears no carry** in portfolio construction. Fees are charged on the fee-paying commitments only, and only the LPs' share of distributions runs through the waterfall — the GP entity's stake comes back in full. Construction used to run the whole fund's distributions through the waterfall (while scaling calls to the LPs' share), which charged carry on the GP's own money; carry now comes out at 20% of the LPs' gain. Carry already paid is read from the books (carry distributions); where the books record none and there is no preferred return — distributions recorded net, the carry paid outside these books — it is worked out from the LPs' distributions beyond their capital (rate ÷ (1 − rate) of the excess), so only carry still to come is forecast.
- **The Analyst can fix an entry it names by its short id.** Its edits quoted the 8-character id the journal shows and failed with "Entry to edit not found"; a short id that names exactly one entry on the entity now resolves.
- **One set of forecasting rules, everywhere.** The construction page, its simulation, the forecast sheet and a GP entity's or management company's forecast all compute through the same fee model, schedule and waterfall, so the GP exemption and carry already paid reach every one of them. The construction page says when fees exclude the GP's stake and when carry already paid was worked out. The rules are written once (`lib/forecast/principles.ts`) and attached to every Analyst tool that drafts a forecast or explains construction, so it reasons the way the engine computes; *Draft with AI* asks it to include construction flows.
- **Edit the forecast by hand.** A new *Entries* tab lists every entry the plan compiles to — date, description, lines, and where it came from (a rule, your amount, an opening balance, construction, or you). Construction and opening entries can be edited (your version replaces the generated one) or removed; a rule's month takes a new amount; anything else can be added as a balanced entry. Clicking a period heading, or a figure on the cash statement, opens the entries behind it. Edits are kept on the plan (`forecast_plans.adjustments`), so a refresh recompiles around them; an edit whose generated entry construction no longer produces is flagged. Migration `20261010300000_forecast_adjustments.sql`.
- **Construction's outcome range is on totals — how much and when.** The cash-flow chart's per-year P10–P90 band understated both: with exit timing varied, a single exit's range in any one year is mostly "did it land this year", so the band sat below the point forecast. The simulation now reports, across runs, total distributions over the forecast (and carry), the year half the money is back, and the share of runs that return nothing; the chart shows each as a range against the forecast. Each run varies the exit amount (lognormal around the expected value, with the loss rate) as well as its timing.
- The forecast toolbar is one row — plan chips (when there are several), view, range, interval, then Refresh, a settings button and New plan — with the plan title and the books-closed line gone.
- **Change a forecast by typing a figure.** On the Cash statement (monthly, working draft), the forecast months of Investments, Exit proceeds, Capital contributions and Distributions take a new amount directly; the entry behind it is written for you — shaped like the one it replaces (an exit keeps its cost; the gain takes the rest), or on the chart's standard accounts when there was none — and shows on the Entries tab. Typing again changes the same entry; 0 clears the month. The P&L already worked this way.
- **The Analyst can make the same edits.** `update_forecast_plan` takes `cashFigures` (a line's cash in a month — the plain way), hand `entries` (balanced, in account codes; with `replaces` to stand in for a generated one), `removeEntries` and `restoreEntries`; the approver sees each change. `forecast_explain` lists the editable entries with their keys.
- **An approved Analyst draft shows up straight away.** Approving a drafted or changed forecast in the Analyst panel reloads the forecast page and opens the new plan, without refreshing the browser.
- The forecast P&L chart no longer carries a net income line; the toolbar is two rows (plan and actions, then view controls); the version select appears only once a version is published; the "not closed" warnings are one line of status; and the forecast's notes are one panel, each note dismissable.
- **"Already paid (prepaid)" cash timing.** A fee paid before the plan — Bluefish's ten-year management fee — is expensed monthly against the prepaid balance with no cash in the plan. The Analyst used to approximate it by pushing the payment 60 or 120 months back, which validation refuses (`cashTiming.months must be a whole number from -36 to 36`).
- The Analyst's proposal card named a plan with an end month a "12-month rolling forecast"; it now says what it is.
- The Forecast page's *New plan* button matches the toolbar's other buttons, and the plan dropdown is gone: nothing with no plan, the plan's name with one, a chip each to switch between several.

### Added — audit and controls
- **An audit trail for the books** (`accounting_audit_events`, append-only — the database refuses edits and deletes). It records who did what, when and why: posting, unposting, voiding and reversing entries (one at a time, in bulk, or from the bank page); period closes (each with its checks and trial balance, so a re-close no longer erases the last review), reopens and approvals; commitment changes (with the event as it was); LP position edits; reports shared and unshared; capital calls issued; distributions declared; K-1s generated, issued and amended; book-to-tax runs; Analyst proposals approved or rejected; interim statements published with `force`. Unlike the activity log, a fund cannot switch it off. Each entity's Admin page shows the latest changes, and the whole trail downloads as CSV for an auditor. Events with partner figures or carry are shown only to members who may read them.
- **Reasons are required** to unpost, reverse or void a posted entry, to ignore a posted bank transaction, to reopen a closed period, and to change or delete a recorded commitment. Discarding a draft needs none.
- **Closes are prepared and approved by different people.** A close now produces a *prepared* review; a second member approves it on the Periods page (one click approves every period awaiting approval). When only one member can do the books, they may approve their own close, and the record says so. LP statements cannot be published for a period until its close is approved (`force` still publishes an interim statement, and is recorded).
- **The close's partner-allocation check is real.** It used to be hard-coded to pass; it now checks that all net income through the period end has reached partners' capital.
- **Reports shared with investors are locked.** The portal reads a shared report's figures live, so editing one silently changed what investors had been shown. Its positions' figures can no longer be edited or deleted, and the report cannot be re-dated or deleted, until it is unshared — enforced in the database as well as the routes.

### Fixed
- **LP statement publishing checked another entity's close.** The closed-period check matched any entity's period in the fund.
- **Editing an initial commitment's amount** lost its *initial* marker (the route never read the event's kind).
- **`/funds/forecast` answered "no forecast vehicle".** The forecast section had no firm-wide landing; it now lists the entities like every other section, and a test fails if a section is added to the nav without one.
- **The Analyst could finish with a blank reply.** It had 2,000 output tokens per round for its thinking, its answer and any plan it staged; a 60-month forecast draft ran out mid-way and the run ended with no text and nothing staged. It now has room for a full draft and more rounds, never returns an empty reply (it says it ran out of room or steps, and what it staged), and the panel shows what it is doing between steps ("Reading your request", "Working out the next step from what it found") rather than going quiet.

### Fixed — tax
- **K-1 Item L is now on a tax basis.** The book-to-tax run reversed unrealized appreciation at fund level only, so every partner's tax capital still held their share of the marks and Item L reported book capital. The reversal now comes out of each partner's capital account, by what the close allocated them. A partner whose tax capital still holds appreciation (adjustments not run, or the books moved since) blocks issuing the package with a message saying so. Item L gains an **Other increase (decrease)** line for transfers between partners, which used to fail the roll-forward check.
- **Unrealized currency translation is reversed for tax**, like unrealized appreciation: out of each partner's capital and off each company's translation account (1250), so tax capital holds none of it until the position is sold. The unrealized mark is now reversed per company account too, rather than all against the parent 1200.
- **Currency translation reaches partners as currency translation.** Both allocation paths filed 4300 under operating income, so a partner's FX movement showed as income on the roll-forward and skewed the K-1 tie-out. It is now its own category, allocated on the partner's terms for unrealized gains. (No production books hold FX entries, so nothing booked needs restating.)
- **The K-1 loader no longer counts receivables as capital.** Per-partner Due from LPs, distributions payable and advances carry a partner id; counted as capital, a call's receivable cancelled the contribution it was raised for.
- **Realized carry stays on the GP's K-1.** The tax book reversed all of the year's carry; it now reverses only the part earned on unrealized appreciation (the carry target measured with and without each partner's marks), so carry on realized gains reaches §1061 and box 20.
- **Each fund's K-1 reads its own portfolio.** Gain character, dividends and qualified dividends were read across the whole firm, so one fund's sales and dividends landed on another's K-1s.
- **The book-to-tax run reads every posting.** It stopped at the first 1,000, understating larger funds.
- **Draft distributions are not in box 19.**
- **Underlying-fund K-1s are tracked per vehicle.** One fund's missing K-1 no longer blocks another fund's tax-year close.
- **Converted SAFEs and notes keep their holding period.** Units received on conversion are held from the instrument's purchase date; only capitalised interest starts a new clock — a conversion no longer turns a long-term gain short-term.
- **Issuing a K-1 package re-checks it against the books.** If anything moved since the draft was generated, issuing is refused until it is regenerated.
- **Reopening a period respects the tax year.** A period inside a closed tax year cannot be reopened until the tax year is; reopening one whose K-1s were issued says so.
- **A tax year's close and reopen history is kept.** Re-closing no longer erases the last reopen's reason; every close and reopen is logged and shown on the Tax page.
- **The tax package includes K-1s only for members who may read partner figures and carry,** matching the K-1 pages.
- **GP and management-company books:** the book-to-tax run finds the unrealized accounts by type, not by code 4200 (which is fee or interest income on those charts).

### Added
- **Advances, charges and deductions on capital calls and distributions.** A partner's wire that arrives before anything is called now waits in a new liability, *2350 Capital contributions received in advance*, instead of being booked straight to capital (which made the call that followed ask for the same money again); issuing a call applies it, so the partner's line is met from the advance and only the rest is due. A call can carry other charges a partner owes (late interest, expenses, fees), each booked to the account it belongs to and settled with the call; a distribution can carry deductions (fees, tax withheld, an unpaid call netted off), which count as paid so only the remainder is wired. The issue form shows each partner's unpaid amount from earlier calls, their advance, and what is due now; notices itemize the charges, the advance applied, earlier unpaid amounts and the total due (or the net paid). Nothing new is stored — charges, deductions and applied advances are their own journal entries, read back by the register (`lib/accounting/call-extras.ts`).
- **Capital calls read and review in Carta's layout.** The call form takes the file itself (.xlsx or .csv) as well as a paste, and reads Carta's capital-activity export as is: *Contribution* is the call, *Capital Received* is recorded as paid, *Prepaid Contributions Applied* is met from the partner's advance on the books (or recorded as received when the books hold none), and *Outstanding Balances Applied* is checked against what earlier calls still have unpaid, with a note when the two disagree. Each issued call has a **Review** that lays it out in the same columns — commitment, contribution, prepaid applied, received, earlier balances, other charges, total due, post-call unfunded and % — and downloads as .xlsx.
- **The entity's Admin page carries its actions in one row** — add an investment or company, import documents, issue a call, declare a distribution.

- **The books follow the schedule of investments, entry by entry.** Every entry derived for a holding — a purchase, a conversion, a mark, an exit — now also books whatever separates the books from the investment records for that holding on that date, as a revaluation (1200/4200), folded into the entry's own lines (`recordsGap`, `lib/accounting/from-portfolio.ts`). The schedule of investments, the Investments page, company pages, LP letters and the Claude/ChatGPT tools now add value-change marks recorded after the latest price (one on or before it is already in the price), which is exactly the ledger's cost-plus-marks; the demo fund's schedule now ties to its books to the cent.
- **A holding with share counts but no share price was valued at $0.** With no price on any investment or mark, every round was valued at shares × a missing price, which threw away its recorded marks (AutoFi read $0 instead of $7.3M). Such rounds are now valued at remaining cost plus their marks; a round that cost nothing (warrants) keeps its marks unless it was actually exited; and the roll-up sorts transactions itself, so a mark read before its round's investment is no longer dropped.
- **ChatGPT could not sign in** ("OAuth authorization response is missing the expected issuer"). The authorization server advertised RFC 9207 but its redirects never carried `iss`; every authorization response now does.
- **An LP invite that failed to send read as "Invited".** Supabase refuses to invite an address it already knows, and the route logged the refusal to the console and returned success. Every invite path (single, bulk, authorized users) now goes through one helper: with an outbound email provider configured, the login is created silently and the invite goes out from the fund's own address with the durable welcome link, so the project-wide Supabase invite template is no longer part of the LP flow; without a provider, Supabase's invite is the fallback. Each attempt is recorded in the LP delivery log and reported honestly. Bulk invites list the addresses that could not be emailed instead of counting them as sent.
- **Activation is now recorded** as the LP's first login, so the activity log no longer starts at their second visit.
- **LP uploads are scanned.** The portal upload, the batch sorter and its confirm, and the admin document upload (which had never scanned) now run the same safety check as every other inbound file; a hit is deleted from storage and refused with the reason.
- **A sent-back item is emailed to the LP** with the fund's note and a link to the checklist, logged as a delivery; an LP upload emails the fund's admins as well as landing in the inbox. Before, both sides had to notice on their own.
- QuickBooks mapping no longer reads "Operating Expenses" as the operating bank account — "operating" was a cash keyword, which mapped the largest expense account on an operating entity's books confidently to Cash
- **Navigation on a phone no longer feels stuck.** Tapping a section did nothing visible until the new page had been fetched and rendered on the server — the old page just sat there, looking live. Every page now paints a skeleton on the next frame, and because the App Router only prefetches a dynamic route as far as its nearest loading boundary, the tab bar's destinations now warm themselves ahead of the tap instead of prefetching nothing at all
- **One auth round trip per page instead of two.** The app shell and the page under it each asked the auth server to validate the session, serially, before any of the page's own queries could start; they now share one check per request. Section guards and the pages behind them likewise resolve access once rather than twice
- Panels and menus open in 200ms rather than 500 — the sheet was animating for half a second, which reads as the app thinking rather than as a panel arriving
- **The installable icon is sharp, and lighter.** The mark was drawn at a fractional scale and centred on a half pixel, so most of its ink rasterised as half-covered grey — worst at the sizes a phone actually installs. Its geometry and its stroke are now snapped to whole (and even) pixels at every size, and the app offers an exact icon for each install slot (152/167/180 for iOS, 192/384/512/1024 for Android and desktop) instead of leaving the platform to resize one. The stroke is also about a third thinner than the toolbar glyph it derives from, which at home-screen size was closing up the drawing; the 32px favicon keeps the heavier weight it needs
- **Mobile navigation works.** The menu drawer had no scroll container and the page behind it is locked while it is open, so on a phone roughly the bottom third of the menu — Settings, Support, most sub-pages — could not be reached at all. Sheets now scroll
- Sub-pages no longer disappear from the phone menu for anyone who had collapsed the sidebar on their desktop: collapsing is a desktop preference and no longer applies to the drawer
- "Underlying funds" and the fund-of-funds ledger pages now appear in the nav for a fund that holds a fund — the flag that switches them on was resolved and then dropped before it reached the sidebar

Removed
- **The shared-account demo.** `/demo` no longer signs visitors into a demo account: the public demo is www.hemrock.com/demo, a static build of the app's own pages on a recorded snapshot, with no session and no database. Gone with it: the `startDemo` action, the middleware exception for `/demo`, `DEMO_USER_EMAIL` / `DEMO_USER_PASSWORD`, the viewer's "Viewing demo" banner and the guard that signed viewers out when a tab closed — which, with the demo gone, would only have signed out real read-only members. The `demo_sessions` table is left in place; nothing writes to it.
- **Dropbox file storage.** Google Drive (and "None / database only") remain; the Dropbox connect flow, settings, and stored credentials are gone
- **Ollama and Google Gemini AI providers.** Anthropic, OpenAI, and OpenRouter remain
- **Email-routing detail surface** — the Email Audit log, the Routing Accuracy dashboard, and the routing confidence-threshold / model-override settings. Inbound email is still auto-classified; only the audit/accuracy dashboards and their tuning settings are gone
- **Fund cash-flow paste import** on the Import page. Fund cash flows now come from the fund accounting feature (posted as journal entries, or via a pasted journal-entry or bank/spreadsheet import), not a direct cash-flow paste
- **GP associates look-through (legacy).** The old `lp_associates_overrides` batch model was already superseded by the live look-through derived from the ledger and `vehicle_gp_links`
