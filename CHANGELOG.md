# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Forecast and portfolio construction
- **Construction flows now carry fees and expenses too.** With *Construction flows* on, a fund's forecast takes construction's management fees and partnership expenses on any account without its own rule (a rule — a prepaid fee, a known invoice — still wins, and says what construction would have put there).
- **A GP entity's forecast comes from its fund's.** With *Construction flows* on, a GP entity forecasts its share of the fund's capital calls and LP distributions (its commitment over the fund's) and its cut of the carry the fund's waterfall pays (its percent of the carry recipients), in the months of the fund's forecast exits — instead of from a construction model of its own.
- **A draft says when its sources have moved.** Changing portfolio construction (the entity's own, a linked fund's for a management company's fees, the fund's for a GP entity) now marks the draft out of date and offers Refresh, as a new close already did. Published versions never change.
- **The GP's own stake pays no management fee and bears no carry** in portfolio construction. Fees are charged on the fee-paying commitments only, and only the LPs' share of distributions runs through the waterfall — the GP entity's stake comes back in full. Construction used to run the whole fund's distributions through the waterfall (while scaling calls to the LPs' share), which charged carry on the GP's own money; carry now comes out at 20% of the LPs' gain. Carry already paid is read from the books (carry distributions); where the books record none and there is no preferred return — distributions recorded net, the carry paid outside these books — it is worked out from the LPs' distributions beyond their capital (rate ÷ (1 − rate) of the excess), so only carry still to come is forecast.
- **The Analyst can fix an entry it names by its short id.** Its edits quoted the 8-character id the journal shows and failed with "Entry to edit not found"; a short id that names exactly one entry on the entity now resolves.
- **One set of forecasting rules, everywhere.** The construction page, its simulation, the forecast sheet and a GP entity's or management company's forecast all compute through the same fee model, schedule and waterfall, so the GP exemption and carry already paid reach every one of them. The construction page says when fees exclude the GP's stake and when carry already paid was worked out. The rules are written once (`lib/forecast/principles.ts`) and attached to every Analyst tool that drafts a forecast or explains construction, so it reasons the way the engine computes; *Draft with AI* asks it to include construction flows.
- **"Already paid (prepaid)" cash timing.** A fee paid before the plan — Bluefish's ten-year management fee — is expensed monthly against the prepaid balance with no cash in the plan. The Analyst used to approximate it by pushing the payment 60 or 120 months back, which validation refuses (`cashTiming.months must be a whole number from -36 to 36`).
- The Analyst's proposal card named a plan with an end month a "12-month rolling forecast"; it now says what it is.
- The Forecast page's *New plan* button matches the toolbar's other buttons, and the plan dropdown is gone: nothing with no plan, the plan's name with one, a chip each to switch between several.

### Added — audit and controls
- **An audit trail for the books** (`accounting_audit_events`, append-only — the database refuses edits and deletes). It records who did what, when and why: posting, unposting, voiding and reversing entries (one at a time, in bulk, or from the bank page); period closes (each with its checks and trial balance, so a re-close no longer erases the last review), reopens and approvals; commitment changes (with the event as it was); LP position edits; reports shared and unshared; capital calls issued; distributions declared; K-1s generated, issued and amended; book-to-tax runs; Analyst proposals approved or rejected; interim statements published with `force`. Unlike the activity log, a fund cannot switch it off. Each entity's Admin page shows the latest changes, and the whole trail downloads as CSV for an auditor. Events with partner figures or carry are shown only to members who may read them.
- **Reasons are required** to unpost, reverse or void a posted entry, to ignore a posted bank transaction, to reopen a closed period, and to change or delete a recorded commitment. Discarding a draft needs none.
- **Closes are prepared and approved by different people.** A close now produces a *prepared* review; a second member approves it on the Periods page (one click approves every period awaiting approval). When only one member can do the books, they may approve their own close, and the record says so. LP statements cannot be published for a period until its close is approved (`force` still publishes an interim statement, and is recorded).
- **The close's partner-allocation check is real.** It used to be hard-coded to pass; it now checks that all net income through the period end has reached partners' capital.
- **Reports shared with investors are locked.** The portal reads a shared report's figures live, so editing one silently changed what investors had been shown. Its positions' figures can no longer be edited or deleted, and the report cannot be re-dated or deleted, until it is unshared — enforced in the database as well as the routes.

### Fixed
- **LP statement publishing checked another entity's close.** The closed-period check matched any entity's period in the fund.
- **Editing an initial commitment's amount** lost its *initial* marker (the route never read the event's kind).
- **`/funds/forecast` answered "no forecast vehicle".** The forecast section had no firm-wide landing; it now lists the entities like every other section, and a test fails if a section is added to the nav without one.
- **The Analyst could finish with a blank reply.** It had 2,000 output tokens per round for its thinking, its answer and any plan it staged; a 60-month forecast draft ran out mid-way and the run ended with no text and nothing staged. It now has room for a full draft and more rounds, never returns an empty reply (it says it ran out of room or steps, and what it staged), and the panel shows what it is doing between steps ("Reading your request", "Working out the next step from what it found") rather than going quiet.

### Fixed — tax
- **K-1 Item L is now on a tax basis.** The book-to-tax run reversed unrealized appreciation at fund level only, so every partner's tax capital still held their share of the marks and Item L reported book capital. The reversal now comes out of each partner's capital account, by what the close allocated them. A partner whose tax capital still holds appreciation (adjustments not run, or the books moved since) blocks issuing the package with a message saying so. Item L gains an **Other increase (decrease)** line for transfers between partners, which used to fail the roll-forward check.
- **Unrealized currency translation is reversed for tax**, like unrealized appreciation: out of each partner's capital and off each company's translation account (1250), so tax capital holds none of it until the position is sold. The unrealized mark is now reversed per company account too, rather than all against the parent 1200.
- **Currency translation reaches partners as currency translation.** Both allocation paths filed 4300 under operating income, so a partner's FX movement showed as income on the roll-forward and skewed the K-1 tie-out. It is now its own category, allocated on the partner's terms for unrealized gains. (No production books hold FX entries, so nothing booked needs restating.)
- **The K-1 loader no longer counts receivables as capital.** Per-partner Due from LPs, distributions payable and advances carry a partner id; counted as capital, a call's receivable cancelled the contribution it was raised for.
- **Realized carry stays on the GP's K-1.** The tax book reversed all of the year's carry; it now reverses only the part earned on unrealized appreciation (the carry target measured with and without each partner's marks), so carry on realized gains reaches §1061 and box 20.
- **Each fund's K-1 reads its own portfolio.** Gain character, dividends and qualified dividends were read across the whole firm, so one fund's sales and dividends landed on another's K-1s.
- **The book-to-tax run reads every posting.** It stopped at the first 1,000, understating larger funds.
- **Draft distributions are not in box 19.**
- **Underlying-fund K-1s are tracked per vehicle.** One fund's missing K-1 no longer blocks another fund's tax-year close.
- **Converted SAFEs and notes keep their holding period.** Units received on conversion are held from the instrument's purchase date; only capitalised interest starts a new clock — a conversion no longer turns a long-term gain short-term.
- **Issuing a K-1 package re-checks it against the books.** If anything moved since the draft was generated, issuing is refused until it is regenerated.
- **Reopening a period respects the tax year.** A period inside a closed tax year cannot be reopened until the tax year is; reopening one whose K-1s were issued says so.
- **A tax year's close and reopen history is kept.** Re-closing no longer erases the last reopen's reason; every close and reopen is logged and shown on the Tax page.
- **The tax package includes K-1s only for members who may read partner figures and carry,** matching the K-1 pages.
- **GP and management-company books:** the book-to-tax run finds the unrealized accounts by type, not by code 4200 (which is fee or interest income on those charts).

### Added
- **Advances, charges and deductions on capital calls and distributions.** A partner's wire that arrives before anything is called now waits in a new liability, *2350 Capital contributions received in advance*, instead of being booked straight to capital (which made the call that followed ask for the same money again); issuing a call applies it, so the partner's line is met from the advance and only the rest is due. A call can carry other charges a partner owes (late interest, expenses, fees), each booked to the account it belongs to and settled with the call; a distribution can carry deductions (fees, tax withheld, an unpaid call netted off), which count as paid so only the remainder is wired. The issue form shows each partner's unpaid amount from earlier calls, their advance, and what is due now; notices itemize the charges, the advance applied, earlier unpaid amounts and the total due (or the net paid). Nothing new is stored — charges, deductions and applied advances are their own journal entries, read back by the register (`lib/accounting/call-extras.ts`).
- **Capital calls read and review in Carta's layout.** The call form takes the file itself (.xlsx or .csv) as well as a paste, and reads Carta's capital-activity export as is: *Contribution* is the call, *Capital Received* is recorded as paid, *Prepaid Contributions Applied* is met from the partner's advance on the books (or recorded as received when the books hold none), and *Outstanding Balances Applied* is checked against what earlier calls still have unpaid, with a note when the two disagree. Each issued call has a **Review** that lays it out in the same columns — commitment, contribution, prepaid applied, received, earlier balances, other charges, total due, post-call unfunded and % — and downloads as .xlsx.
- **The entity's Admin page carries its actions in one row** — add an investment or company, import documents, issue a call, declare a distribution.

## 0.9.7 — 2026-10-09

Highlights — a large release; everything since 0.9.6 (July 2026):

- **Accounting is always on.** Every entity keeps double-entry books from creation; investments, marks, exits and capital calls post as they are recorded, P&L is allocated to partners as each entry posts, and the Accounting switch only decides whether the pages show.
- **Access by entity.** Members see the funds, SPVs and entities granted to them — across the app, the Analyst, MCP and the database — on top of per-area grants.
- **Forecast.** Budgets, rolling forecasts and variance per entity, with AI-drafted plans from each account's own history.
- **Claude and ChatGPT.** A plugin built for your deployment, OAuth sign-in, and live dashboards inside the conversation.
- **One Entities section** for funds, SPVs, GP entities, individuals and management companies; LP onboarding documents; holdings, digital assets and fund-of-funds positions; and charts across Investments, Partners and Entities.

Added
- **Forecast: budgets, rolling forecasts and variance per entity.** A budget is a fiscal year planned by month, published as an immutable version and approved as the baseline; a rolling forecast runs 12/18/24/36 months from the last closed month and moves with the books. One rule per income/expense account — manual, fixed, recurring schedule (an annual bill lands in its month, never smoothed), historical run rate over closed months only, growth, seasonal profile, linked management fee, portfolio construction — with month overrides that keep the rule. Cash timing by double entry (accruals, prepayments, deferred revenue; same month, offset, a calendar month, or a billing cycle), so P&L and cash diverge as they would in the books. Views by month, quarter or year over any range: P&L, a cash-flow statement (operating by account, investing, financing, each cash account), Actual + forecast, Variance against the approved budget or any version, charts for ending cash, revenue/expenses, cash flows by source and variance, and CSV. Fee links (`manco_fee_links`) tie a management company's fee revenue to each fund's construction fee schedule on a billing cycle; a fund plan can carry construction's investments, exits, calls and distributions in real months. Stored as balanced entries in `forecast_*` tables (migrations `20261009500000_forecast_planning.sql`, `20261009600000_forecast_seasonal_rule.sql`), never in the ledger. Feature switch **Forecast** (`budgeting`) under Fund Operations; ten MCP/API tools; Analyst staged actions to create, update and publish a plan.
- **AI-drafted forecasts.** *Draft with AI* asks the Analyst for a forecast; it starts from a rule suggested for every account from 12–36 closed months of its own history (recurring bills, seasonal shapes, trends, step changes, fixed amounts, one-offs — each with evidence and a confidence; marks and gains are never forecast from history), adjusts for what you tell it, and stages one plan for approval. The same suggestions seed *New plan*.
- **Access by entity.** A second axis beside the domain grants: each member sees **All entities** (including future ones and unassigned items), specific funds/SPVs/entities, or none — set in Settings → Team → Access, and chosen when approving a join request. Companies, positions, deals, diligence, LPs, notes, documents, emails, metrics, the review queue, accounting, the Analyst, agent tools, MCP and API keys all follow it; a shared company shows each member only their entities' side. Enforced at the API, on every server page, and by restrictive row-level security (migrations `20261007100000`–`20261008030109`). Admins see everything; existing members were given All entities; new members start with none.
- **The Analyst shows what it is doing.** An elapsed timer and each step as it runs (✓ done, ✗ failed) replace a static "Thinking…", on /start and in the sidebar. `/api/analyst` streams progress as NDJSON when asked; the JSON response is unchanged otherwise.
- **Post from the Close page.** Drafts inside the month and suggested recurring entries can be posted where the close lists them (*Post*, *Post all*, *Post selected*), through the journal's guarded bulk post.
- **Bank paste straight from a bank's website.** A transaction table copied off a bank site — one cell per line, descriptions wrapping onto two lines, extra columns (Quantity, Price, Balance), invisible zero-width characters — now imports like a CSV.
- **Charts on the Investments, Partners and Entities pages.** Each page now leads its table with two charts drawn from the same rows the table lists, so every filter, search and as-of date applies to both. *Investments*: fair value and cost by holding (the bar is the carrying value, the tick is current cost, so a markup runs past the tick and a markdown stops short of it), and invested capital against total value by gross multiple (below 1x through 10x and up, with total value split into proceeds received and fair value still held). A holding owned through several vehicles is one bar. *Partners* and *Entities*: called against committed (the solid bar inside the tint is what has been drawn), and total value against called capital (distributions, then NAV, with a tick at capital called), switchable between dollars and multiples; the Entities page opens on multiples and follows the net-to-LP lens. Charts list the ten largest with the rest folded into one "others" row that expands in place. The row form is the one the fund page's Portfolio chart uses, extracted to `components/ranked-bars.tsx`; the arithmetic is in `lib/charts/` with tests. Chart cards now keep one header height whether or not they carry a toggle, so side-by-side cards line up.
- **Capital calls and distributions: by percentage, from a spreadsheet, and from the entity's Admin page.** Issue a call as an amount or a percentage of commitments. Paste a call or distribution from a spreadsheet — a partner column, an amount, and optionally what was paid and when — and the form fills each partner's line for review; issuing records the call and posts the receipts the sheet already shows (the same cash-against-receivable entries a bank match posts), each tagged so a retry posts nothing twice (`lib/accounting/register-import.ts`). An entity's Admin page now carries the create actions (add investment or company, import documents, issue a call, declare a distribution) and a Capital calls card with each open call's status.

The moment any of its holdings has a figure, the holdings take over, so nothing counts twice (`lib/portfolio/deal-vehicles.ts`). The holdings chart switches between fair value and gross multiple, here and on the Claude/ChatGPT portfolio dashboard (holdings under 1.0x in the loss colour). A field left empty means the fund-wide pacing or Monte Carlo setting; a stated one wins for that deal alone, so a late-stage position can hold a tighter, sooner exit than the seed checks around it. The forecast section lists the resulting deal timeline, and the simulation runs as soon as any one deal states a spread
- **Distributions declare through the waterfall.** A fund-wide distribution used to split by capital balance across every partner, GP included. The panel shows the tiers, a suggested K-1 character and any warnings before you declare; carry posts as its own `carry_distribution` entry against the accrued carry; the register records the method and each tier (migration `20260919000000`). Pro-rata by capital remains for vehicles with no carry terms, and as an explicit choice

The cards lead with it; the LP agent's capital-calls tool reports it

A distribution notice states the partner's share of return of capital and preferred return when the waterfall produced the split

- **A delivery log** (`lp_deliveries`, migration `20260919000002`): one row per email to an LP — notice, receipt, statement, letter, document, announcement, reply — with the address, the Cc, the provider's message id, and a failed row when the provider refused. `GET /api/lps/deliveries` reads it per item

- **Unfunded calls in the daily digest**: three days before the due date, on the day, then weekly, naming who is behind
- **Two-way LP messaging**: reply from the LP Portal inbox (emailed, threaded, shown in the LP's portal), announcements to chosen or all LPs with the same address review, and the portal's Contact page shows the conversation (migration `20260919000003`)
- **Calls and distributions on capital-tracking vehicles**: the register row is the call, the notice renders from it, and funding or payment is recorded on the line by hand
