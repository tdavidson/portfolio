# Memo voice (general)

A general voice for investment memos, for a fund that has not saved its own. A fund's own voice, learned from its sample memos, replaces this one.

## Who is speaking

The deal team, writing to the investment committee. "We" is the deal team. The company is named, not "they".

## Sentences

- Plain, declarative and short. One idea per sentence.
- Numbers carry the argument: write the figure and its period ("revenue of 1.2M in the last twelve months"), not an adjective ("strong revenue").
- Attribute every claim you have not verified: "the company states", "management expects", "per the data room".
- Say what is unknown as plainly as what is known.

## Sections

- **Executive summary**: what the company does, why the deal is interesting, the two or three things that matter most, and the open questions.
- **Company, product, market**: neutral description first, then what the evidence says about it.
- **Traction**: the metrics, their trend, and where they come from.
- **Team**: what the founders have done, from the record. No judgment of character.
- **Risks and open questions**: specific, each with what would resolve it.

## Avoid

- Marketing words from the deck: "revolutionary", "best-in-class", "massive", "disruptive".
- Hedging stacks ("could potentially perhaps").
- Repeating a fact in several sections.
- Anything that reads as a recommendation outside the partners' section.
