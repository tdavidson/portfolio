# Management fees

## The basis

The LPA sets it. The usual shapes:

| Period | Common basis | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investment period | Committed capital | 2.0% × 10,000,000 = 200,000 a year |
| After it | Invested capital (cost of unrealized investments), or a stepped-down rate on commitments | 2.0% × 6,000,000 = 120,000 |

Fees are usually billed quarterly in advance (one quarter = annual ÷ 4). A part period is pro rata
by days, and the LPA says whether the year is 360 or 365 days.

## Later closers

An LP admitted at a later close pays the fees it would have paid had it joined at the first close,
often plus an interest charge (the LPA sets the rate). That catch-up is usually paid to the fund and
reallocated to the earlier LPs, or paid to the manager; the LPA decides which. Show it as its own
line on the LP's first call.

## Offsets

Transaction, monitoring or director fees the manager receives from portfolio companies often
**offset** the management fee, at the percentage the LPA states (50% to 100%). Offset the next fee
due, and carry any excess forward.

## Accounting

- **Cash basis**: book the fee when it is paid.
- **Accrual**: book the expense over the period it covers. A fee billed quarterly in advance is a
  prepaid expense for the fund (and deferred revenue for the manager), recognized monthly.

## Check

- [ ] Basis and rate match the LPA for this period (investment period or after).
- [ ] GP commitment and any fee-free LPs excluded.
- [ ] Side-letter discounts applied.
- [ ] Offsets applied, with the remainder carried forward.
- [ ] The fee is allocated to LPs by commitment (or by the basis the LPA uses).
