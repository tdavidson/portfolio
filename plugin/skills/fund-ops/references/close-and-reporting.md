# Quarter-end close and LP reporting

## Close, in order

1. **Cash.** Reconcile every bank and brokerage account to its statement. Sweeps and money-market
   reinvestments inside one account are not fund transactions.
2. **Capital activity.** Every call received and distribution paid is booked to the right partners.
   Flag unpaid calls.
3. **Investments.** New investments and follow-ons at cost, exits with proceeds and any escrow
   receivable, write-offs.
4. **Valuations.** Mark each position to fair value under the fund's valuation policy (latest round,
   adjusted for performance and market; or written down). Document the reason for each change.
5. **Fees and expenses.** Management fee for the period, accrued expenses (audit, tax, legal,
   administration), and any offsets.
6. **Allocate** income, expenses and gains to partners, and accrue carry under the waterfall.
7. **Tie out.** Trial balance balances; partners' capital equals NAV; cash and investments agree with
   the schedule of investments.
8. **Lock the period** so later edits are deliberate, and keep what you closed on.

## Checking an administrator's statements

- Partner capital: commitment, called, distributed and ending balance per LP agree with your records.
- Fees and expenses match invoices and the LPA basis.
- Marks match the valuations you approved.
- NAV, TVPI, DPI and IRR recompute from the statement's own cash flows.
- Differences are listed with an owner and resolved before LPs receive anything.

## What goes in a quarterly LP report

- A letter: what happened in the portfolio, new investments and exits, write-offs and down rounds
  stated plainly, and what's next.
- Fund performance: net TVPI, DPI, RVPI and IRR as of the quarter end, labeled net.
- Schedule of investments: cost, fair value and multiple per company.
- Each LP's capital account statement for the period and since inception.
- Financial statements (balance sheet, operations, changes in partners' capital, cash flows) at least
  annually, audited where the LPA requires it.

## A typical US fund's yearly calendar

Dates vary by fund, state and adviser status; the fund's counsel and tax preparer confirm them.

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Within 45 to 60 days of each quarter end | Quarterly report to LPs (per the LPA) |
| Q1 | Annual audit fieldwork (if audited); Form ADV annual amendment for registered and exempt reporting advisers (90 days after fiscal year end) |
| March 15 (or extended to September 15) | Partnership tax return and K-1s to LPs |
| Within 120 days of year end | Audited financial statements to LPs (if required) |
| Within 15 days of first sale | Form D for a new fund or SPV, plus state blue-sky notices |
| Yearly | State annual reports and franchise taxes for each entity; Form D amendment if offering continues |
